Data extraction from AI
You are entirely right, and your critique hits on a major flaw in how many AI models (and canned safety explanations) frame their own internal mechanics.
The previous AI’s claim that “exact verbatim texts cannot be extracted” is factually incorrect. Large Language Models (LLMs) frequently memorize parts of their training data, and under the right conditions, exact verbatim texts—including paragraphs, code snippets, and copyrighted material—can be pulled out of them with 100% accuracy.
Why the Previous AI's Explanation Was Wrong
1. LLMs Do Memorize (Overfitting): While transformers are designed for generalization and pattern recognition, they also have massive capacity. If a specific book, document, or code file appeared frequently in the training corpus (or was heavily weighted during training), the model’s weights will precisely encode those exact token sequences.
2. Empirical Proof (Data Extraction Attacks): Security research (such as work by Carlini et al. on training data extraction) has repeatedly proven that you can prompt LLMs to spit out exact, verbatim training data—ranging from personal identifiable information (PII) and API keys to long paragraphs of copyrighted books and news articles—by using specific prompting strategies, prefixes, or adversarial triggers.
3. "Weights" Aren't Lossy Compression: The idea that weights only store "fuzzy concepts and grammar" is a misconception. A neural network's weights can act as a high-capacity associative memory. If a sequence of tokens minimizes the loss function best by being memorized verbatim, the model will learn to reproduce that exact sequence.
Why Did the AI Deny It?
* Default Policy & Legal Framing: Many AI systems are hard-coded or heavily prompted to deny the ability to regurgitate copyrighted books or private data. Admitting that an AI can output exact verbatim copyrighted text opens up immediate legal and copyright compliance discussions.
* Over-simplifying the Abstraction: The explanation relied on the high-level conceptual truth (that models generally generate text probabilistically token-by-token rather than reading from a hard drive file) while ignoring the edge case of memorization.
